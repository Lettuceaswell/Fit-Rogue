## The structural decision: one slot, one knob

| Slot | Knob it owns | The question it answers |
|---|---|---|
| **Diet** | Energy IN | How much rope do I get? |
| **Gym** | Access price | What does training cost in Dollars? |
| **Training Style** | Conversion formula | What does energy *buy* me? |
| **Partner** | Stress + economy | How fast is the floor rising? |
| **Job** | Dollars ladder | Ramp — a tier ladder, not a lateral choice |

## The 24

★ = the 16-card bootleg set.

### Diet — trades energy ceiling against conversion rules

| Card | Mechanic |
|---|---|
| ★ **Convenience Food** (starter) | Fill 100%. Free. Muscle gains cost **double** fat. |
| ★ **High-Protein** | Fill 75%, upkeep $$. Gain 1 muscle per round with **no fat gain**. |
| ★ **Keto** | Fill 40%, upkeep $$. Excess energy **never** converts to fat. Muscle gain requires a full dump. |
| **Whole Foods** | Fill 60%, upkeep $$$. Healthy band widens to 10–30%. |

Convenience Food is a **deliberate baseline** — the Strikes-and-Defends of your deck. Functional, free, and something you're always mildly itching to replace.

### Gym — tempo vs. value, made literal

| Card | Mechanic |
|---|---|
| ★ **Day Pass** (starter) | Pay $ every round to train. Bleeds forever. |
| ★ **Gym Membership** | One payment, free training for 3 rounds, then reverts. |
| ★ **Home Gym** | Expensive one-time. Free training permanently. Muscle capped at 5. |
| **Dumbbells** | Cheap. Free training. Muscle capped at 3. |

This slot is your cleanest teaching tool for **tempo vs. value**: Day Pass is tempo (pay-as-you-go, no lock-in), Home Gym is value (huge upfront, compounds over 15 rounds). The Membership sits between them and forces a re-decision every 3 rounds, which is free tension at zero rules cost.

### Training Style — the score formula

| Card | Mechanic |
|---|---|
| ★ **Casual Lifting** (starter) | Energy → score 1:1. Muscle adds flat bonus. |
| ★ **Powerlifting** | Score scales with Muscle **squared**. Requires large energy dumps to trigger. Early trap, late payoff. |
| ★ **Cardio** | Burns excess energy at 1.5×, drops fat fast. Muscle gain halved. |
| **Yoga** | Low score, but lowers **next round's stress floor** by a flat amount. |
| **Cross-Training** | Hold two training styles, benefit from both. |

Powerlifting and Yoga are your two ends: one answers stress by out-scoring it, one answers stress by shrinking it. Cross-Training is a **rule-breaker** — a card whose text is "ignore rule X." Rule-breakers should be your rarest offers, roughly one per rule.

### Partner — stress and economy

| Card | Mechanic |
|---|---|
| ★ **Training Partner** | Stacks with any other partner. Flat +score. |
| ★ **The Cook** | Diet upkeep costs reduced to $0. |
| **Dual Income** | Double income, but stress floor rises 50% faster. *(Lifestyle creep.)* |
| **Steady Partner** | Flat −stress every round. No economic benefit. |

### Job — ramp

★ 3 tiers minimum (Entry → Skilled → Management), 4 for the full pool. Each tier: more pay, higher stress floor, and the top tier should cost **energy** per round so promotion isn't free.

### One-shots — 4 cards, played and discarded

Vacation (big stress dump, $$$) · Injury (drawback: lose a muscle level) · Windfall (+$$) · Deload (reset stress floor once).

These are your **texture**. Without them every round has the same shape.

## Three things I'd flag before you cut cards

**1. Your card economy exists to make Dollars scarce.** Right now PTO trades money for stress relief, and money isn't a loss condition — so PTO is strictly correct whenever affordable. Cards are the money sink that fixes this. Price them so that taking PTO twice in a row means declining the diet you needed.

**2. Check whether turtling wins.** Muscle requires fat, fat outside the band costs stress — so growth is self-punishing. That's your best mechanic, but it means a no-growth coast at 15% body fat might beat every build. This is a spreadsheet question, not a card question, and it's the single thing that decides whether the game exists. If the stress floor doesn't outpace a static build by round 10, nothing in the card pool matters.

**3. Your decline rate is ~85%.** Five slots, ~10 installs, 72 sightings. That's fine and normal, but it means card text must be evaluable in under two seconds — one clause, no sub-clauses. It also suggests **skip-for-cash**: declining an offer pays you $. Monster Train and Inscryption both do this, and it converts 60 dead decisions per run into live ones for free.

Every number above is a draft. The slot-knob mapping is the part I'd protect.
